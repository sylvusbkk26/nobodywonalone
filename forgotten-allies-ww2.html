The forgotten Allies

The war against Hitler was not fought by three nations. Poles broke Enigma, Indians formed the largest volunteer army in history, and French colonial troops opened the road to Rome.

8 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerDozens of nations fought Nazi Germany. Among the most important contributions beyond the "Big Three": Poland's codebreakers and airmen, the Free French and their African troops, the armies of India, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, and the resistance movements of occupied Europe, above all in Yugoslavia.

Poland: first to fight, first to break Enigma

Poland was the first country to resist Hitler by force, in September 1939. Its cryptologists Marian Rejewski, Jerzy Różycki and Henryk Zygalski had been reading Enigma since 1933. In July 1939 they gave their methods to Britain and France, saving the British at least a year, according to GCHQ.

Polish pilots were the largest foreign group in the Battle of Britain (145), and their No. 303 Squadron was its highest-scoring unit. In August 1944, about 45,000 fighters of the Polish Home Army rose against the Germans in Warsaw. The uprising lasted two months and cost more than 180,000 lives.

France: 1940, Italy, Provence

France did fight in 1940: nearly 60,000 soldiers were killed in under six weeks. From 1943, the French Expeditionary Corps of General Juin, made up largely of Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian and Senegalese troops, fought in Italy. After American assaults on Monte Cassino failed, the French broke through the mountains at the Garigliano in May 1944, opening the road to Rome.

In August 1944, French Army B, recruited largely in Africa, made up most of the force that landed in Provence. Toulon and Marseille fell about a month ahead of schedule. The Resistance sabotaged railways and telecommunications before and after D-Day; the official US Army history called it a "bonus" planners could not count on, but a real one.

The Commonwealth

  • Canada: more than a million served and over 42,000 died. Canadians took Juno Beach and later cleared the Scheldt to open the port of Antwerp.
  • India: the Indian Army grew from about 205,000 men in 1939 to more than 3.35 million in 1945, often described as the largest volunteer army in history. More than 87,000 died.
  • Australia: nearly a million served and 39,000 died. Australians held Tobruk under siege in 1941.
  • New Zealand: the highest death rate per head of population in the Commonwealth.

Yugoslavia, Norway and the resistance

Yugoslav partisans grew from about 81,000 fighters in late 1941 to more than 800,000 in spring 1945, losing 245,549 killed in action. With the Red Army, they liberated Belgrade in October 1944. In 1944 they also helped evacuate hundreds of downed American airmen.

In Norway, the 1943 Gunnerside raid destroyed about 500 kg of heavy water. Its effect on Germany's atomic program is hard to measure, since Germany was far from building a bomb, but it remains one of the war's most daring operations.

The fair versionMany of these forces fought with American and British weapons: France alone received $3.2 billion in Lend-Lease. That is not a footnote against them. It is the coalition working as it should.

Frequently asked questions

Who broke the Enigma code?

Polish cryptologists broke Enigma from 1933 and shared their methods with Britain and France in July 1939. British codebreakers at Bletchley Park, including Alan Turing, built on their work.

How many Indian soldiers fought in World War II?

The Indian Army grew to more than 3.35 million men by 1945, often described as the largest volunteer army in history.

Did France fight in World War II after 1940?

Yes. French forces fought in North Africa, Italy, Provence, France and Germany, many of them recruited in North and West Africa.

Which countries were the Allies in World War II?

The main Allies were the UK and its Commonwealth, the USSR, the US, China and France, alongside Poland, Yugoslavia, Greece, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium, Czechoslovakia and many others.
