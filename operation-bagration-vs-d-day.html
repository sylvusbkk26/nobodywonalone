Bagration: the battle you never heard of

On June 22, 1944, sixteen days after D-Day, the Red Army attacked in Belarus. Within two months, Germany had lost one of its largest army groups.

6 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerOperation Bagration was the Soviet summer offensive of 1944 in Belarus. It destroyed about 30 German divisions and inflicted around 450,000 losses on the Wehrmacht in two months. It happened at the same time as the Normandy campaign, and against a much larger share of the German army.

What happened

The offensive opened on June 22, 1944, three years to the day after Germany invaded the Soviet Union. It was aimed at German Army Group Centre, which held a huge bulge of territory in Belarus. Soviet deception had convinced the Germans that the main blow would fall further south, in Ukraine.

By August 19, the Red Army had advanced hundreds of kilometers to the borders of East Prussia and the Vistula river in Poland. About 30 German divisions were destroyed. The Imperial War Museum puts German losses at about 450,000 men, and Soviet losses at over 770,000.

The comparison is not a contest. The two offensives worked together: Germany could not send reserves West without weakening the East, and the reverse. But in terms of the German forces destroyed, Bagration was the larger blow.

"Perhaps the greatest single win"

American military historian Robert Citino has described Bagration as "perhaps the greatest single win in all of military history", as quoted by the Imperial War Museum. It is a striking verdict from a US historian about a battle most Americans have never heard of.

Why the West forgot it

During the Cold War, Soviet victories were rarely taught or celebrated in the West, and the USSR's own accounts were seen as propaganda. D-Day had the cameras, the films and later the anniversaries attended by US presidents. Bagration had none of that in the West.

The fair versionBagration was made possible in part by American trucks supplied through Lend-Lease, which gave the Red Army the mobility to advance so fast. And D-Day opened the second front Stalin had demanded since 1942. The two offensives of summer 1944 belong in the same story.

Frequently asked questions

What was Operation Bagration?

The Soviet offensive in Belarus from June 22 to August 19, 1944, which destroyed German Army Group Centre.

Was Bagration bigger than D-Day?

In terms of German forces involved and destroyed, yes. The Soviets faced about three quarters of the German army in summer 1944, while the Allies in Normandy faced less than a quarter.

Why is Operation Bagration not well known?

Cold War politics and Western films and commemorations focused attention on D-Day. Soviet victories were rarely taught in Western schools.

Did Bagration and D-Day happen at the same time?

Yes. D-Day began on June 6, 1944 and Bagration on June 22, 1944. The Normandy campaign and Bagration both ran through August 1944.
