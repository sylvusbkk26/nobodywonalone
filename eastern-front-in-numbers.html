The Eastern Front in numbers

The largest land war in history was fought between Germany and the Soviet Union. This is where most of the German army died, and where the Soviet peoples paid a price no other Ally came close to.

7 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerAbout 4 million of Germany's 5.3 million military dead fell fighting the Soviet Union. Soviet military deaths were at least 8.7 million, and total Soviet losses, civilians included, are officially put at 26.6 million. Some of those numbers come with important caveats, explained below.

German losses: the Overmans count

The standard reference is the study by German historian Rüdiger Overmans, published in 2000. He counts 5,318,531 German military dead. Up to the end of 1944, 2,742,909 died on the Eastern Front and 339,957 in the West, about eight to one. The 1.23 million who died in the final battles of 1945 are not split by front. His overall estimate is about 4 million dead in the East and about 1 million in the West.

In June 1941, 99 of Germany's 190 divisions attacked the USSR. In May 1944, on the eve of D-Day, 62% of the German army was still in the East. The often-quoted claim that 90% of German forces were in the East is wrong: most of the Luftwaffe and the navy fought the Western Allies.

According to Ohio State University's Origins, more Soviet soldiers died at Stalingrad in five months than Americans died in the whole war.

Soviet losses, and why the numbers kept changing

Soviet death tolls have always been political. Stalin announced 7 million dead in 1946. Khrushchev raised it to 20 million in 1961, and Gorbachev to "nearly 27 million" in 1990. The figure used by Russia today is 26.6 million, from a demographic study by Andreev, Darski and Kharkova.

For military deaths, the official minimum is 8,668,400, from a study led by Grigori Krivosheev, including about 1.28 million who died as prisoners. Independent estimates run from about 10.7 million to about 13.9 million.

Two cautions. First, 26.6 million is a total population loss, which includes victims of Soviet repression and Soviet citizens who died serving Germany: it is not a count of people killed by Germans. Second, figures of 40 million and more circulate online but are not taken seriously by most researchers.

The fair versionThe Red Army did most of the killing and most of the dying in the land war against Germany. It also fought with American trucks, fuel and rail equipment, and it was led by a regime that had been Hitler's partner until June 1941. And the USSR was not only Russia: Ukrainians, Belarusians, Kazakhs and many other peoples fought and died in its ranks.

Frequently asked questions

How many German soldiers died on the Eastern Front?

About 4 million of Germany's 5.3 million military dead, according to historian Rüdiger Overmans.

How many Soviet soldiers died in World War II?

At least 8.7 million according to the official Russian study led by Grigori Krivosheev. Independent estimates go up to about 13.9 million.

Is it true that 27 million Soviets died?

The official figure is 26.6 million total population loss, civilians included. It also includes victims of Soviet repression and is not a count of deaths caused by Germany alone.

What was the bloodiest battle of World War II?

Stalingrad, with more than 1.8 million total casualties, is usually considered the bloodiest single battle.
