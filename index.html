Bagration, the battle you never heard of

Two weeks after D-Day, the Red Army launched Operation Bagration in Belarus and destroyed about 30 German divisions in two months. In summer 1944, the Soviets faced roughly three quarters of the German army; the Allies in Normandy faced less than a quarter.

Imperial War Museum

The part Moscow leaves out

From 1939 to 1941 Stalin was Hitler's partner. The USSR invaded eastern Poland, annexed the Baltic states, shot about 22,000 Polish prisoners at Katyn and shipped Germany oil and grain. Any honest account starts there.

German–Soviet Commercial Agreement, 1940; Katyn records

03 / 1939–1941

Before Pearl Harbor: help, but no war

Washington sent more and more aid to Britain, but it refused to fight. In January 1940, 88% of Americans opposed declaring war.

  1. Aug 1935 – Nov 1939Neutrality Acts

    Congress bans arms sales to nations at war, then allows them on a cash-and-carry basis, which in practice favored Britain and France.

  2. Sep 1939Britain and France go to war

    The US stays neutral. Poland is invaded by Germany, then by the USSR on September 17.

  3. 10 & 13 June 1940France asks. America says no.

    Prime Minister Paul Reynaud begs Roosevelt to enter the war at once. Without it, he warns, France will seek an armistice. It does, on June 22.

  4. Jul – Oct 1940Britain stands alone

    The RAF wins the Battle of Britain. One pilot in five is not British.

  5. 2 Sep 1940Destroyers for bases

    50 old US destroyers are traded for 99-year leases on British bases.

  6. 11 Mar 1941Lend-Lease becomes law

    America becomes the "arsenal of democracy". The USSR is added in October.

  7. 22 June 1941Germany invades the USSR

    99 of Germany's 190 divisions attack in the East.

  8. Sep – Oct 1941Undeclared naval war

    US escorts fire on U-boats. The USS Reuben James is sunk on October 31.

  9. 7 & 11 Dec 1941Pearl Harbor, then Hitler's choice

    Japan attacks. Four days later, Germany declares war on the United States, and Congress answers the same day.

05 / Fair is fair

What America really gave

This site isn't here to downplay anyone's sacrifice. Without the United States, the war would have lasted longer and ended differently.

  • About 52% of all munitions produced by the Big Three Allies, 1939–1944 (Mark Harrison).
  • $50.1 billion in Lend-Lease, including 427,284 trucks for the Red Army.
  • Marshal Zhukov, recorded in 1963: “The Americans really bailed us out with gunpowder and explosives.”
  • The air and sea war that drained most of Germany's industrial output (Phillips O'Brien).
  • 175,445 US Army combat deaths in Europe and the Mediterranean, including more than 19,000 in the Battle of the Bulge.

Sources: Harrison (1998); Lend-Lease records; The Insider; US Army Final Report on Battle Casualties; National WWII Museum. Did Lend-Lease win the war? →

The poll that flipped

Every nation thinks it won

Asked in 2024 who did most to defeat the Nazis, 59% of Americans named the US and 39% of Britons named the UK. In Russia, 63% believe the USSR would have won without any Allied help.

YouGov 2024; Levada Center 2017

Young Americans already see it

In 2015, only 36% of Americans aged 18–29 said the US did most to defeat Germany, against 66% of those over 65. D-Day's place in memory grew with films and Cold War commemorations.

YouGov US 2015; Le Grand Continent

07 / Both ways

Myths, busted on every side

A fair story has to correct everyone, us included. See all myths →

The American myth

D-Day was the turning point

Moscow (1941), Stalingrad (1943) and Kursk (1943) all came first. By June 1944 Germany had already lost the war in the East.

D-Day was an American invasion

Most of the troops, ships and aircraft were British and Canadian. Americans became the majority in the West afterwards.

America came to Europe's rescue

France asked in June 1940 and was refused. The US went to war with Germany after Hitler declared war on it.

The Soviet myth

Russia won alone

Lend-Lease supplied most of the Red Army's trucks, 58% of its high-octane aviation fuel and 93% of its rail equipment. And the USSR wasn't only Russia: Ukrainians, Belarusians and many others fought and died.

90% of Germany fought in the East

62% of the German army was in the East in May 1944. Most of the Luftwaffe and navy fought the Western Allies.

“English time, American money, Russian blood”

The quote is often attributed to Stalin, but there is no primary source for it. Don't use it.
