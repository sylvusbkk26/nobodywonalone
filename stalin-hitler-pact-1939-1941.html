1939–1941: Stalin's pact with Hitler

The Soviet Union did more than any other nation to destroy the German army. For the first 22 months of the war, it was also Hitler's partner.

6 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerIn August 1939, the Soviet Union signed a non-aggression pact with Nazi Germany. A secret protocol divided Eastern Europe between them. The USSR invaded Poland, annexed the Baltic states and supplied Germany with oil and grain until Hitler turned on Stalin in June 1941.

The pact and its secret protocol

The Molotov–Ribbentrop pact of August 23, 1939 was publicly a non-aggression treaty. A secret protocol attached to it divided Poland, the Baltic states and other territories into German and Soviet spheres. A week later, Germany invaded Poland. On September 17, 1939, the Red Army invaded from the east. In June 1940, the USSR annexed Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania.

Fuel for the German war machine

Under the commercial agreement of February 11, 1940, the Soviet Union supplied Germany with about 820,000 tonnes of oil and 1.5 million tonnes of grain, as well as manganese, chrome and rubber bought abroad for German use. It also gave Germany rail transit to Romania, Iran and Afghanistan. These deliveries continued while Germany conquered France and bombed Britain.

Katyn

In the spring of 1940, the Soviet secret police shot about 22,000 Polish officers, police and intellectuals at Katyn and other sites, on an order signed on March 5, 1940. For fifty years, Moscow blamed Germany, including at the Nuremberg trials. The USSR admitted responsibility only on April 13, 1990.

The Western Allies were not innocent of the silence. Roosevelt buried a report pointing to Soviet guilt, and Churchill's government accepted the Soviet version in public while privately doubting it.

The shadow over 1944–1945

In August 1944, as the Warsaw Uprising began, the Red Army reached the suburb of Praga across the Vistula but did not cross to help. In 1945, Soviet troops committed mass rapes in Germany; one study puts the number of women raped in Berlin alone at around 100,000.

Why this page existsThis site argues that the Soviet contribution to defeating Germany has been underplayed in the United States. Telling that story honestly means telling this one too. A sacrifice of millions does not erase what the Soviet state did, and what it did does not erase that sacrifice.

Frequently asked questions

What was the Molotov–Ribbentrop pact?

A non-aggression treaty signed by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union on August 23, 1939, with a secret protocol dividing Eastern Europe between them.

Did the Soviet Union invade Poland in 1939?

Yes. The Red Army invaded eastern Poland on September 17, 1939, sixteen days after Germany invaded from the west.

Did the Soviet Union supply Nazi Germany?

Yes. Under a 1940 trade agreement, it delivered about 820,000 tonnes of oil and 1.5 million tonnes of grain, among other materials, until June 1941.

Who was responsible for the Katyn massacre?

The Soviet secret police (NKVD), on an order signed by Stalin and the Politburo on March 5, 1940. The USSR admitted it in 1990.
