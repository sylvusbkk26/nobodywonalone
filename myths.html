Myths, busted both ways

A fair account of the war has to correct everyone. Here are the most common myths on every side, checked against the sources.

The American myths

D-Day was the turning point of the war

Moscow (1941), Stalingrad (1942–43) and Kursk (1943) all came before D-Day. By the end of 1944, Germany had lost about eight times more soldiers in the East than in the West. D-Day opened a second front against an army that was already losing. Read more

D-Day was an American invasion

About 53% of the troops who landed on June 6 were British and Canadian, and the Royal Navy supplied 892 of the 1,213 warships. Americans paid the heaviest price on the day and became the majority in the West afterwards. Read more

America rushed to Europe's rescue

France asked for US entry into the war in June 1940 and was refused. The US went to war with Germany on December 11, 1941, after Hitler declared war on it. Read more

Without America, Europe would be speaking German

Germany had already lost the bulk of its army in the East before the US fought a major battle in Europe. Without America, the war would likely have lasted longer and Western Europe's fate could have been very different, possibly under Soviet rather than German control. It is a counterfactual, not a fact. Read more

The Soviet myths

Russia won the war alone

The Red Army fought with 427,284 American trucks, most of its high-octane aviation fuel and almost all its rail equipment from Lend-Lease. Marshal Zhukov admitted in 1963 that the Americans had "bailed us out". And the USSR was not only Russia. Read more

90% of German forces fought in the East

In May 1944, 62% of the German army was in the East. Counting the Luftwaffe and navy, which fought mostly against Britain and the US, the share was lower still. Read more

27 million Soviets were killed by the Germans

26.6 million is the official total population loss. It includes victims of Soviet repression and Soviet citizens who died serving Germany. The Soviet tragedy was immense; the number still needs its context. Read more

The USSR was on the right side from the start

From August 1939 to June 1941, the Soviet Union was Germany's partner: it invaded Poland, annexed the Baltic states, shot 22,000 Poles at Katyn and supplied Germany with oil and grain. Read more

Quotes to avoid

Stalin said: "English time, American money, Russian blood"

There is no known primary source for this line. It does not appear on Stalin's Wikiquote page. Don't use it.

Eisenhower said the Resistance was worth 15 divisions

This line is widely repeated but could not be found in the official US Army history or at the Imperial War Museum. Treat it as unverified. Read more

Other myths

Greece saved Moscow by delaying Barbarossa

A US Army study found that late spring floods ruled out an earlier start anyway, and that the delay came mostly from the Yugoslav campaign. It called the lost weeks of minor significance.
