D-Day: which countries took part?

Twelve or more nations took part in the Normandy landings. Most of the troops, ships and aircraft were British and Canadian. The heaviest losses were American.

5 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerD-Day was led by the United States, Britain and Canada, with forces from Australia, Belgium, Czechoslovakia, France, Greece, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Rhodesia and others. About 53% of the troops who landed on June 6 were British and Canadian, and Britain supplied about three quarters of the warships.

Troops, ships and planes

On June 6, about 73,000 Americans came ashore by sea and air, against about 83,115 British and Canadian troops. At sea, the Royal Navy provided 892 of the 1,213 warships and 112,824 sailors, against 52,889 Americans. About two thirds of the aircraft were British.

The other nations

Free French paratroopers and commandos, Polish and Czechoslovak airmen, Norwegian and Greek ships, and Australian and New Zealand aircrews all took part. In the weeks before the landing, the French Resistance sabotaged railways and telecommunications to slow German reinforcements, and the official US Army history records hundreds of locomotives destroyed in the three and a half months before D-Day.

Canada's role continued long after June 6. In the autumn of 1944, the First Canadian Army cleared the Scheldt estuary, opening the port of Antwerp, which the Allies needed to supply their advance.

The fair versionD-Day was an Allied operation under an American supreme commander, Dwight D. Eisenhower. Americans paid the highest price on the day, and they became the majority of Allied forces in Western Europe in the months that followed.

D-Day in the bigger picture

Two weeks later, on June 22, 1944, the Soviet Union launched Operation Bagration in Belarus, destroying about 30 German divisions. D-Day opened the Western Front; it did not open the war against Germany, which had been going on in the East for three years.

Frequently asked questions

How many countries took part in D-Day?

Forces from at least twelve nations took part, led by the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada, with Australian, Belgian, Czechoslovak, French, Greek, Dutch, New Zealand, Norwegian, Polish and Rhodesian contingents.

Did more Americans or British land on D-Day?

More British and Canadian troops landed combined: about 83,115, against about 73,000 Americans.

Which country lost the most soldiers on D-Day?

The United States. Of 4,414 confirmed Allied dead, 2,499 were American, most of them at Omaha Beach.

Was D-Day the turning point of World War II?

Most historians place the turning points earlier: Moscow in 1941, Stalingrad and Kursk in 1943. By June 1944 Germany was already losing the war in the East.
