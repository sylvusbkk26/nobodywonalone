Why did Hitler declare war on America?

After Pearl Harbor, the United States declared war on Japan, not on Germany. Four days later, Hitler made the choice for it.

6 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerHitler declared war on the United States on December 11, 1941, even though his alliance with Japan did not require it. He believed America was already fighting Germany at sea, hoped Japan would keep it busy in the Pacific, and saw Roosevelt through an antisemitic worldview. Historians widely regard it as one of his worst decisions.

Pearl Harbor did not bring America into the war in Europe

On December 8, 1941, Congress declared war on Japan with a single dissenting vote. It did not declare war on Germany. For two years, Roosevelt had helped Britain with everything short of war, but American public opinion still opposed sending troops to Europe. Polls compiled by historian Eric Rauchway show that throughout 1941, about 70% of Americans answered "stay out" when asked directly whether the US should enter the war.

The treaty did not require it

Germany, Italy and Japan had signed the Tripartite Pact in September 1940. Its third article promised mutual assistance only if a member was attacked. Japan had attacked the United States, not the other way around. Hitler was under no legal obligation to act, as the National WWII Museum points out.

Why Hitler did it

  • The undeclared naval war. Since September 1941, US warships had been escorting convoys and were under orders to fire on German submarines on sight. The destroyer USS Reuben James was sunk on October 31, 1941.
  • Lend-Lease. American weapons and supplies were flowing to Britain, and to the USSR from October 1941.
  • A miscalculation about Japan. Hitler expected Japan to tie down American power in the Pacific for years.
  • Ideology. In his speech to the Reichstag, Hitler portrayed Roosevelt as a tool of "the eternal Jew".

"His greatest mistake"

Historian Martin Gilbert called the declaration Hitler's greatest mistake. Brendan Simms and Charles Laderman argue that December 11 mattered even more than Pearl Harbor for the shape of the war, because it settled the question America's politicians had not: the US would fight Germany, and in fact would put Germany first.

The fair versionAmerica was not neutral in 1941. It was arming Britain and the USSR, and its navy was already shooting at U-boats. But the formal decision to make the United States a combatant in Europe was taken in Berlin, not in Washington.

Frequently asked questions

Did Germany declare war on the US or did the US declare war on Germany first?

Germany declared war first, on December 11, 1941. Congress declared war on Germany and Italy later the same day.

Was Germany obliged to declare war because of its alliance with Japan?

No. The Tripartite Pact only required assistance if a member was attacked. Japan was the attacker at Pearl Harbor.

Would the US have joined the war in Europe without Hitler's declaration?

Probably eventually, given the naval war in the Atlantic and Roosevelt's support for Britain, but historians disagree on how soon. Public opinion in 1941 still largely opposed entering the war.

What did the US do to help Britain before December 1941?

Cash-and-carry arms sales from November 1939, 50 destroyers traded for bases in September 1940, Lend-Lease from March 1941, and armed escort of Atlantic convoys from September 1941.
