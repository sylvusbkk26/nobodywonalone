June 1940: France asked. America said no.

As German armies closed on Paris, France's prime minister sent Roosevelt two desperate appeals. Twelve days later, France signed an armistice.

5 min readSourced and fact-checked
Short answerYes, France asked the United States to enter the war. On June 13, 1940, Prime Minister Paul Reynaud asked whether the US would declare war, saying it was France's only chance. The United States did not, and France signed an armistice with Germany on June 22, 1940.

The two appeals

On June 10, 1940, the day Italy entered the war, Reynaud cabled Roosevelt. He asked the president to declare publicly that the United States would support the Allies "by all means short of an expeditionary force". The cable is preserved by the American Presidency Project.

Three days later, with the government evacuated from Paris, Reynaud went further. US diplomatic records show he asked whether the United States would declare war, and said its immediate entry was the only way to save France. The American diplomat reporting the message warned that, without American action, France might request an armistice within 48 hours.

What happened next

No declaration came. Reynaud resigned on June 16 and was replaced by Marshal Pétain, who requested an armistice. It was signed on June 22, 1940. In less than six weeks of fighting, nearly 60,000 French soldiers had been killed and about 1.8 million taken prisoner, according to the French Ministry of the Armed Forces.

Why America did not act

  • The law. Only Congress can declare war, and the Neutrality Acts of 1935–1939 had been written to keep America out of European wars.
  • Public opinion. In June 1940, only 35% of Americans said they were willing to risk war to help Britain. In January 1940, 88% opposed declaring war.
  • Military weakness. The US Army of 1940 was small. General George Marshall expected Britain to fall next and wanted to keep weapons for the defense of the Americas.
The fair versionRoosevelt was sympathetic, and the United States began sending large-scale aid to Britain within months. But in the hour France needed it most, America chose to stay out. That is a fact worth remembering whenever someone says America "came to Europe's rescue".

Frequently asked questions

Did France ask the United States for help in 1940?

Yes. Prime Minister Paul Reynaud appealed to Roosevelt on June 10 and June 13, 1940, the second time asking for an immediate US entry into the war.

Why didn't the US help France in 1940?

Only Congress could declare war, the Neutrality Acts were in force, most Americans opposed entering the war, and the US Army was not ready.

When did France surrender in World War II?

France signed an armistice with Germany on June 22, 1940, after about six weeks of fighting.

Did France fight in World War II after 1940?

Yes. Free French forces fought in Africa, Italy and France, and French divisions took part in the Provence landing of August 1944 and the liberation of Paris.
